Bistable Paper Origami

Design development and experimental evaluation of Kresling and facet-modified Miura structures

This study examines reversible switching in paper Kresling and facet-modified Miura structures. Both fabricated specimens retain distinct configurations after manual actuation and release.

Literature review, numerical screening and fabrication trials informed the final designs. The recordings provide qualitative evidence of bistable behavior.

Two-page report ↓ PDF

Study protocol

Methods

Design and fabrication

Patterns were adapted for US Letter printing, manual scoring and cutting. Kresling revisions addressed seam interference; Miura revisions reduced fold complexity and localized facet bending. The final Miura used 65 lb cardstock.

Computational screening

Geometric and elastic calculations screened fold compatibility, contact, strain and recovery. Candidate results guided successive design revisions.

Experimental assessment

Manual A–B–A actuation was followed by complete hand release in each configuration. The recordings document shape retention and return motion. Edited footage preserves the original transition speed.

Scope. Numerical models used assumed elastic properties and crease rest angles. The final Miura’s permanent B/D creases were introduced after these simulations. Experimental evidence is based on release observations; forces, energy barriers and endurance were not measured.

01 / Folding complexity

8 × 8 facet sheet

The sheet required extensive coordinated folding. A seeded defect persisted numerically, while direct loading and release returned to the initial state.

02 / Fabrication and response

12-cell curved lens-box

Curved scoring and bonded connectors complicated fabrication. Full-panel loading did not produce switching; a smaller diagnostic patch did not complete the return transition.

03 / Refinement check

6 × 6 straight facets

Straight scoring simplified fabrication, but a coarse-model second state disappeared on refinement. Other thin-sheet equilibria persisted; the finer switching calculation did not converge.

04 / Simplified trial

One-vertex / 2 × 2 facets

Four 45 mm rhombi reduced the folding effort. The initial model returned to A; revised stiffness assumptions produced a numerical cycle. The first physical trial was inconclusive.

05 / Final physical prototype

Diagonal panel hinges

Added diagonals through B and D divided two rhombi into four triangles. The 65 lb cardstock specimen exhibited A → B → A pop-through and retained both configurations after release.

03 / Mechanical interpretation

Mechanical role of auxiliary facet creases

Silverberg’s pop-through mechanism suppresses an original fold and bends adjacent facets. Its single-cell model represents this bending with two symmetric auxiliary hinges, flat at rest. 2 · PDF ↗

The added B/D creases localize facet bending and alter hinge stiffness and rest angles. These changes can reduce resistance to inversion and provide a plausible mechanism for the observed pop-through.

The resulting equilibria depend on geometric compatibility, crease stiffness and fold memory. Nonlinear compatibility can produce multiple energy minima even with simple crease-spring laws. 5 · PDF ↗

Reduced energy description

U(q) = ½ Σ ki[θi(q) − θi,0]2 + Upanel(q)

q = allowed shape coordinates; θ = fold rotations; k = rotational stiffness; Upanel includes remaining panel deformation.
Local equilibrium and stability

∇U(qA) = ∇U(qB) = 0

A sufficient local stability test is a positive-definite constrained energy Hessian at each equilibrium, after removing rigid-body motion. Bistability also entails separation by an energy barrier.

The energy expression provides a qualitative interpretation of the added-crease mechanism.

Recent work on origami-inspired tubes similarly identifies local fold architecture and its constitutive response as design variables governing global stability. 6 · PDF ↗
