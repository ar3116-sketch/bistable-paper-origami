Bistable Paper Origami

Design development and experimental evaluation of Kresling and facet-modified Miura structures

This exploratory study evaluates paper Kresling and Miura-derived structures for reversible transitions between distinct configurations retained after removal of manual actuation.

The workflow combines literature-informed geometry, uncalibrated numerical screening, hand-fabrication revisions, and video-based release observations. The final specimens exhibit behavior consistent with bistability; force–displacement and energy-barrier measurements remain outside the scope of this study.

Study protocol

Methods and evidence classification

Design and fabrication

Candidate patterns were adapted for US Letter printing, manual scoring, scissors, and adhesive where required. Kresling development focused on seam interference and facet continuity. Miura development progressively reduced crease count and localized panel bending. The final Miura used 65 lb cardstock.

Computational screening

Geometric checks and independent elastic models were used to screen candidate equilibria and loading–release paths. Material constants and crease rest angles were not calibrated to the specimens. Failed loading, return, and refinement checks were retained in the design record rather than interpreted as physical validation.

Experimental assessment

Manual actuation was followed by complete hand release. Distinct retained shapes and a return transition were assessed from the recordings. Footage was trimmed, color-corrected and annotated without altering transition speed. The evidence supports qualitative bistable behavior; it does not measure the potential-energy landscape.

01 / Folding burden

8 × 8 facet sheet

The large sheet required many coordinated folds. A seeded numerical defect survived, but a direct press-release trial returned to the starting shape. A seeded endpoint did not establish an accessible transition.

02 / Manufacturing + model limit

12-cell curved lens-box

The 4 × 3 design used curved lens creases and glued Miura connectors. It was difficult to score accurately by hand; the full-panel simulations did not switch on release. A smaller diagnostic also failed the clean reverse transition.

03 / Refinement check

6 × 6 straight facets

Straight scoring simplified fabrication. A coarse-model second state disappeared after mesh refinement under the same assumptions. Other thin-sheet cases retained minima, but the finer switching calculation did not converge.

04 / Simplified trial

One-vertex / 2 × 2 facets

The 45 mm rhombi retained the Miura vertex while reducing folding work. One illustrative model returned to A; later stiffness choices produced a conditional numerical cycle. The first filmed physical attempt was inconclusive.

05 / Final physical prototype

Diagonal panel hinges

Adding P-to-corner creases through B and D divided two rhombi into four triangles. The 65 lb cardstock specimen then showed a visible A → B → A pop-through cycle and retained both configurations after release.

03 / Mechanical interpretation

Mechanical role of auxiliary facet creases

Silverberg’s pop-through mechanism suppresses an original fold and bends adjacent facets. Its single-cell model represents this bending with two symmetric auxiliary hinges, flat at rest. 2 · PDF ↗

Here, permanent creases concentrate that deformation along B and D instead of demanding smooth bending across each entire panel. This can reduce the resistance to inversion and alter the resting configuration of the panels. That is a plausible explanation for the improvement observed in the cardstock specimen.

The modification introduces extra folding freedom; more freedom alone does not create two stable states. Their existence depends on geometry, hinge stiffness and fold memory. Elastic origami can have several energy minima even when each crease has a simple spring law. 5 · PDF ↗

Reduced energy description

U(q) = ½ Σ ki[θi(q) − θi,0]2 + Upanel(q)

q = allowed shape coordinates; θ = fold rotations; k = rotational stiffness; Upanel includes remaining panel deformation.
Local equilibrium and stability

∇U(qA) = ∇U(qB) = 0

A sufficient local stability test is a positive-definite constrained energy Hessian at each equilibrium, after removing rigid-body motion. Bistability also entails separation by an energy barrier.

This is a mechanical interpretation, not a fitted energy curve for the final specimen. Creasing changes stiffness and rest angle; the published flat-rest hinge idealization is not automatically the same material model. Earlier simulations informed the trial but did not simulate the final added creases. The filmed release test is the evidence for the finished piece.

Related work on origami-inspired tubes distinguishes crease-network geometry from the constitutive response of local folds. This supports treating fold architecture as a separate design variable; it does not validate the present paper specimen. 6 · PDF ↗
